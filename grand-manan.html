Three Days on Grand Manan: Puffins, Whales, and Sea Fog

July 17–19, 2026 · Grand Manan Island, NB

The trip started early — a 7 a.m. rental pickup and a drive to the Blacks Harbour ferry terminal. I cut it close and rolled in about 30 minutes before departure, which meant parking in the non-reserved lane and boarding after everyone else. Ninety minutes later, Grand Manan came into view.

Day 1 — Coastal Landmarks

The walk out to Swallowtail Lighthouse starts with the trail opening up ahead — sea on one side, the lighthouse itself in the distance.

The trail opening up toward Swallowtail Lighthouse.

Further along, the passage itself — a couple of hikers on the boardwalk for scale.

The passage out to the lighthouse.

Near the top, another angle felt like Grand Manan distilled into one frame — cliff, sea, and a line of trees in the foreground.

Cliff, sea, and trees — Grand Manan in one frame.

A couple minutes' drive further, along Net Point Trail, the sea opened up from an elevated point where a group of seals were playing in the water below — too far off for a usable shot, but worth remembering. That's also where the ferry shot at the top of this post was taken.

From there, a hike out to the Hole in the Wall — a 12-metre basalt arch — turned into a longer detour than planned when the trail back wasn't the one expected. Instead of heading straight to the parking lot, it led out to Fish Head first, then back.

The Hole in the Wall, and the detour out to Fish Head.

Late afternoon was a quiet stretch of sea combing along the shore — that's also where the snail eggs turned up, tucked along the tide line. Nothing from the combing made the cut for photos, but it was a good way to slow down after the hike. Wrapped up the day back at the cottage, watching the sunset from the deck with a hot coffee.

Day 2 — Wildlife and High Cliffs

The morning started with a boat out to Machias Seal Island, after a brief scheduling mix-up with the tour organizers got sorted.

The island's sign board, and the boat dropping everyone at the landing point.

Shooting happened from inside a wooden blind, tucked right at the edge of the colony.

The blind we shot from.
Puffins & Razorbills

From there, puffins and razorbills came and went constantly — I shot this on a mixed focal range, primarily at the longer end, to isolate each bird from the background.

A puffin returning with a catch.
A puffin at rest, a razorbill on the rocks, and a puffin mid-call.
Razorbills lined up, an unlikely rock-mate, and one on its own.
The colony at a wider view, three puffins sharing a rock, and one last close-up.
Seal Island & the Whale Watch

A short crossing over, Seal Island came into view, its old lighthouse tower rising over the rocks.

Seal Island and its tower.

From there we boarded a smaller boat for the whale-watching leg, everyone lined up along the railing scanning the water.

On the railing, watching for the first sign of a whale.

A few humpbacks surfaced nearby — first just the tail slipping under, then a clearer look at it a moment later.

A humpback's tail slipping under, then a closer look.

At one point, what looked like a mother and calf surfaced close to the boat, giving a real sense of scale.

Mother and calf, with the boat for scale.

A pair of seabirds skimmed low across the water while we watched, wingtips breaking the surface.

Two seabirds gliding across the water mid-whale-watch.
Southwest Head

The afternoon closed at the Southwest Head lightstation, where volcanic cliffs drop straight into the ocean.

Southwest Head — volcanic cliffs meeting the sea.
Day 3 — Fog and the Long Way Home

The last morning rolled in thick with fog. A stretch of curvy road along the way made for a quick portrait backdrop, though none of those shots made the final cut here.

Fog sat heavy over Dark Harbour — boats anchored just offshore, a pickup truck parked along the water's edge.

Dark Harbour — boats in the fog, and the harbour split between sea and rock.

The next stop was Stanley Beach, near the ferry terminal that would carry us back to New Brunswick. A Kelp Gull came in low, hunting for crab along the shoreline.

A Kelp Gull coming in to hunt.
Crab caught, wings folding in.
Holding on as the waves built up behind it.

One last stop after the ferry back: Reversing Falls, the bridge arching over the water.

Reversing Falls, to close out the trip.

Across the three days, I moved through the Tamron's full range depending on what the scene called for — wide to hold the whole cliffside or coastline in frame, tight to pick out a single bird mid-motion. Everything was shot RAW, so there's room to fine-tune light and colour before the strongest frames make it into the main gallery.
